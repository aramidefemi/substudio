Why we built it

It started by watching
one person work.

Substudio began with a friend who subtitles Nollywood films for a living, mostly Yoruba films translated into English. She listens to every line of dialogue, translates it, and times it to the frame. A single film can run past a thousand subtitles.

She does it in Subtitle Edit, an excellent open-source tool with decades of work behind it. But watching her screen, we saw how much of a long film is spent on the same few motions: set the start, set the end, nudge, play it again, fix the line, move on. Every extra click is multiplied by a thousand.

AI hasn't solved this either. Yoruba is rich in proverbs, humour, tone and context that speech and translation models still flatten. Throwing a language model at a film doesn't produce subtitles a professional would put her name to.

So we didn't start with another AI translator. We started with the editor, because it has to be genuinely good without AI.

She shares recordings of how she works, tells us what gets in her way, and tests every build. Substudio grows from that, not from guesses about what a subtitle editor should be.

What it's for

Not the editor with the most features.
The best one for long films.

Built for the whole film

Two hours of audio and 1,000+ lines. The list, the waveform and the video stay fast from the first line to the last.

Hands stay on the keyboard

Every action has a shortcut, with a Subtitle Edit preset so years of muscle memory carry over. ⌘⇧K finds anything else.

Timed to the frame

Timecodes in frames at 25 fps by default. Stepping and snapping use the film's real frame times, so timing holds on any file.

Private by default

The films are unreleased. Everything runs on your Mac and nothing is uploaded, ever, without asking you first.

Yoruba is first-class

Tone marks and dotted letters (ẹ̀, ọ́, ṣ) render correctly everywhere, and spell check learns your names and places.

The tools she already uses

Waveform timing, hold-to-time, script import from a Google Doc, quality checks, burn-in and SRT export. The familiar workflow, with less friction.

Where it's going

The editor first.
Then help that respects the craft.

  1. Now

    A complete manual workflow

    Everything needed to subtitle a full film without the mouse getting in the way. This preview is that editor.

  2. Next

    A whole film, by choice

    The real test: she subtitles an entire film in Substudio because she wants to, not because we asked. Every rough edge she hits comes first.

  3. Later

    Drafts you correct, not answers you accept

    Yoruba-to-English drafts and translation memory, shaped by years of real subtitling work. AI proposes, the subtitler decides, and the waveform stays where the final word is set.

It starts with one Nollywood subtitler. We hope it becomes useful to subtitlers, translators and filmmakers working in every language.

Installing

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Substudio into Applications.
  2. Open Substudio. This preview isn't notarised by Apple yet, so macOS stops it the first time. Click Done.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to Substudio. Confirm once and you won't be asked again.
Comfortable with Terminal?

Instead of steps 2–3, run this once after copying the app to Applications:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Substudio.app
